Three conclusions follow directly.

Google is the entire market. At roughly 93% share, optimising for anything else in Sri Lanka is a rounding error. Every ranking decision that matters is Google's.

Demand is still expanding. 1.5 million Sri Lankans came online in a single year and 9.37 million still haven't. Search volume in this country has not plateaued. Categories that look small today will not be small in three years, which means positions won cheaply now become expensive to displace later.

Your traffic is on a phone. With mobile connections at 130% of the population, most visits arrive on mid-range Android devices over mobile data. A site that scores well on office fibre and badly on a Dialog connection in Kurunegala is losing customers it never sees.

What Broke: Google Now Answers Half the Questions Itself

This is the shift that separates SEO companies that updated their methods from ones still selling 2019 tactics.

AI Overviews — the generated summary block above the blue links — moved from experiment to standard feature in roughly two years. The independent measurements:

  • Prevalence. BrightEdge tracked AI Overviews on approximately 48% of queries as of February 2026, up around 58% year on year. More conservative mixed-intent datasets put the figure at 15–25%. Informational and question-shaped queries trigger them far more often than commercial ones.
  • Click impact. Ahrefs measured a 58% drop in click-through rate for the top organic position when an AI Overview is present (February 2026). Pew Research, tracking 68,879 real searches, found 8% of users clicked an organic result with an AI Overview present versus 15% without — and only about 1% clicked the citation links inside the summary.
  • Zero-click share. Between roughly 58% and 65% of all Google searches now end without a click to any website, depending on dataset.

Now the number that matters commercially: Semrush found brands cited inside AI Overviews earn roughly 35% higher organic click-through than brands that aren't cited. And citation overlap with the organic top ten has fallen sharply — the pages Google quotes in AI answers are increasingly not the pages ranking first.

Being cited has become a separate visibility layer from ranking. An SEO company that only reports keyword positions is now measuring one of two scoreboards.

The Unclaimed Opportunity: Sinhala and Tamil Search

Almost nobody in this market is competing for Sinhala or Tamil queries. Per-keyword volumes are lower, but competition is close to zero, cost per acquisition is far lower, and intent is stronger — someone searching in Sinhala for a local service is rarely comparison-shopping international sites.

Done correctly it requires native writing rather than machine translation, proper hreflang implementation (si-LK, ta-LK, en-LK), and separate URL paths rather than a JavaScript language switcher. Prioritise the pages where language changes buying behaviour: services, pricing, contact and FAQs.

This is one of the highest-return, lowest-competition plays available to a Sri Lankan business in 2026, and it is why our own homepage speaks Sinhala.

Red Flags: When to Walk Away

  • "Guaranteed #1 rankings." Nobody controls Google's ranking systems.
  • "Thousands of backlinks." Volume-based link building has been devalued for years. Bulk directories, PBNs and paid placements on unrelated sites are still sold aggressively in Sri Lanka and still carry risk.
  • Reports that are only rank screenshots. Positions are an input. Enquiries are the output.
  • No technical capability. If they cannot touch code, half of modern SEO is unavailable to them.
  • Hundreds of near-identical location pages. Thin duplication across city names is a liability, not a strategy.
  • No mention of AI search. With AI Overviews on a substantial share of queries, a provider who hasn't adjusted is optimising for a version of Google that no longer exists.

A 20-Minute Vendor Evaluation

Before you sign with anyone, including us:

  1. Ask for three live client domains. Search their target keywords yourself. If they won't name clients, stop.
  2. Ask who executes the work and whether you speak to them directly.
  3. Ask how links are acquired, in specific terms. Bulk directories, PBNs or paid guest-post networks in the answer is a warning.
  4. Ask to see a sample monthly report. Look for conversions, not just positions.
  5. Ask what they will change in the first 30 days and why those items first.
  6. Ask how they track AI search visibility as a distinct channel.
  7. Confirm the legal entity and address.

The answers to those seven questions will tell you almost everything.
